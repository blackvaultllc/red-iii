HOW TO SPOT A FAKE EMAIL

Fake emails look real on purpose. Here is how to tell them apart.

Daily article — November 5, 2026  |  Silent Security Vault — Domenick Arlon Hall, Mobile, AL

A fake email looks like it came from a company you know. It may have the right logo and the right colors. The giveaway is almost always in the details — who sent it, where it wants you to go, and what it asks you to do.

What you need to know

  • Read the sender address. Tap the name to see the real address. A real bank does not write from a free email address with extra letters in it.
  • Hover before you click. On a computer, hold your mouse over a link and look at the address at the bottom. If it does not match the company, do not click.
  • Watch for urgent threats. 'Your account will close today' is pressure, not service. Real companies give you time.
  • Check the greeting. A real company usually uses your name. 'Dear customer' plus a demand for money is a warning sign.
  • Attachments are a trap. Do not open an attachment you did not expect, even from a name you know. Call the person first.
